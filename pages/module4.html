4. AC & inverters

What is alternating current?

Alternating current (AC) is electricity whose direction of flow reverses periodically, tracing a smooth sinusoidal wave. Unlike DC — which flows in a single constant direction — AC voltage rises from zero to a positive peak, falls back through zero to a negative peak, and repeats. One complete cycle per second equals 1 Hz.

Grid frequency is 50 Hz in most of Europe, Africa and South America, and 60 Hz in North America. Household outlets deliver 120 Vrms or 230 Vrms depending on the region.

💡 Why AC for the grid? AC voltage can be stepped up to hundreds of kilovolts with a transformer for long-distance transmission, then stepped back down near homes. This dramatically reduces resistive losses.

How an inverter converts DC to AC

A solar inverter uses a set of fast-switching transistors (MOSFETs or IGBTs) arranged in an H-bridge. By alternately switching pairs of transistors, the inverter reverses the polarity of the DC many thousands of times per second. A filter then smooths this high-frequency pulsed output into a clean sine wave at 50 or 60 Hz.

This switching strategy is called Pulse Width Modulation (PWM). Modern inverters achieve total harmonic distortion (THD) below 3 %, meeting grid codes worldwide. Conversion efficiency typically reaches 95–98 %.

⚡ MPPT — Maximum Power Point Tracking. The inverter continuously samples the panel voltage and current to find the exact operating point where power output (P = V × I) is highest. As clouds pass or temperature changes, the MPPT algorithm adjusts within milliseconds.

Types of inverters

The three main inverter types suit different applications. Choosing correctly affects system cost, reliability and the ability to store or export energy.

Grid-tie Grid-connected

Syncs with the utility grid. No battery required. Exports surplus power (net metering). Requires anti-islanding. Shuts down if grid fails.

Off-grid Standalone

Operates independently. Requires a battery bank. Creates its own AC reference. Used for remote sites and areas without reliable grid access.

Hybrid Battery + grid

Combines grid connection and battery storage. Can prioritise solar self-consumption, discharge batteries during peak-rate hours, and charge from the grid overnight.

String inverters vs microinverters vs power optimisers

Within the grid-tie family, system designers choose between a central inverter for the whole array, per-panel microinverters, or a hybrid approach with DC optimisers.

String inverter

One inverter for the whole array. Lowest cost and easiest to maintain. Entire string limited by the weakest panel — shading or mismatch hurts total output.

Microinverter

One inverter per panel. Panel-level MPPT eliminates shading losses. No single point of failure. Higher upfront cost; best for complex or partially shaded roofs.

Power optimiser

DC–DC optimiser per panel feeds into a single string inverter. Achieves panel-level MPPT at lower cost than microinverters.

Anti-islanding safety

Anti-islanding is a mandatory safety feature for all grid-tie inverters. If the utility grid goes offline — due to a fault or deliberate shutdown for maintenance — the inverter must disconnect within milliseconds. Without this protection, the inverter would continue energising the lines, creating a dangerous "island" of live voltage that could electrocute utility workers.

⚠️ Grid codes mandate anti-islanding. Standards such as IEC 62116 (international) and UL 1741 (USA) define the maximum trip time — typically under 2 seconds, often under 0.16 seconds. Never connect a grid-tie inverter that does not comply with your local grid code.

Key terms

AC Alternating Current — electric current that periodically reverses direction, described by a sine wave.
Inverter Power electronics device that converts DC (from panels or batteries) into AC for loads and the grid.
MPPT Maximum Power Point Tracking — algorithm that adjusts the inverter's input voltage to maximise PV array output.
Grid-tie Inverter configuration that synchronises with the utility grid and requires anti-islanding protection.
Anti-islanding Safety mechanism that disconnects the inverter from the grid within milliseconds if the grid fails.
THD Total Harmonic Distortion — measure of sine wave purity. Grid-tie inverters must achieve THD < 3 %.
