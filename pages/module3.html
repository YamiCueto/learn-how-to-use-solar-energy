3. DC circuits

40 min Requires Module 2 Active

What is direct current?

Direct current (DC) is the flow of electric charge in a single direction — unlike alternating current (AC), which reverses direction 50 or 60 times per second. Solar panels, batteries and charge controllers all operate on DC. Understanding DC circuits is the core of any solar installation.

In a DC circuit, electrons flow out of the negative terminal of the source, through the loads, and back to the positive terminal. Voltage (V) drives that flow; resistance (Ω) opposes it; and current (A) describes how many coulombs pass per second.

💡 Key insight. PV panels generate DC. To power AC appliances or feed the grid you need an inverter that converts the system's DC into 120 V or 230 V AC.

Ohm's Law: V = I × R

Ohm's Law is the fundamental equation of any electric circuit. It relates three quantities: voltage (V), current (I) and resistance (R). If you know any two, you can always find the third.

V = I × R
V Voltage
(volts)
I Current
(amperes)
R Resistance
(ohms)

Rearrange it as needed: I = V / R for current; R = V / I for resistance. This simple formula governs your panel wiring, cable losses and inverter behaviour.

Practical example: a string delivers 48 V and feeds a 5.3 Ω load. Current = 48 / 5.3 ≈ 9.1 A; power = 48 × 9.1 ≈ 437 W.

Power in DC circuits

Power (P, in watts) describes how fast energy is transferred. In DC it combines directly with Ohm's Law:

P = V × I
P Power
(watts)
V Voltage
(volts)
I Current
(amperes)
  • P = V × I — the basic power formula.
  • P = I² × R — useful for calculating cable losses when you know the current.
  • P = V² / R — useful when you know voltage and resistance.
⚡ Energy vs. power. Power (W) is an instantaneous rate. Energy (Wh) is power × time. A 1 000 W array running for 5 peak-sun hours produces 5 000 Wh = 5 kWh.

Series wiring

Wiring panels in series — positive of one to the negative of the next — forms a string. Voltages accumulate while current stays the same as a single panel.

Series string
Vtotal = V₁ + V₂ + …
Itotal = I (unchanged)
  • Vtotal = V₁ + V₂ + V₃ + …
  • Itotal = I of one panel (unchanged).
  • Example: 4 × 40 V / 10 A → 160 V, 10 A, 1 600 W.
⚠️ Shading alert. If a single cell in a string is shaded it acts as a resistor and can drag down the whole string's output. Module optimisers or micro-inverters mitigate this effect.

Parallel wiring

Wiring strings in parallel — positive to positive, negative to negative — adds the currents while keeping the voltage constant. This combining is done at a combiner box.

Vtotal = V₁ + V₂ + …
Itotal = I (unchanged)
  • Voltage ↑ — Current →
  • Higher voltage → longer cable runs
Parallel
Vtotal = V (unchanged)
Itotal = I₁ + I₂ + …
  • Vtotal = voltage of one string (unchanged).
  • Itotal = I₁ + I₂ + I₃ + …
  • Example: 2 × 160 V / 10 A → 160 V, 20 A, 3 200 W.
🔒 Blocking diodes. In parallel circuits, blocking diodes prevent a shaded string from consuming power from the others.

Voltage drop in wiring

Every cable has electrical resistance. When current flows through it, voltage is lost: Vdrop = I × Rcable. This loss heats the cable and reduces the power available at the inverter.

For solar systems, the standard recommendation is to keep voltage drop below 2–3 % of the string voltage. Use larger-gauge cable for longer runs or higher currents.

📐 Quick rule. Doubling the cable's cross-section halves its resistance and therefore halves the voltage drop. A 6 mm² cable has half the resistance of a 3 mm² cable.

Key terms

Voltage (V) Potential difference between two points, in volts (V). Drives current flow.
Current (A) Rate of electric charge flow, in amperes (A). 1 A = 1 coulomb per second.
Resistance (Ω) Opposition to current flow, in ohms (Ω). R = V / I by Ohm's Law.
Power (W) Rate of energy transfer, in watts (W). P = V × I in DC circuits.
Series Components wired end-to-end: current flows through each in sequence; voltages add up.
Parallel Components wired between the same two nodes: currents add; voltage is the same.
Voltage drop Voltage lost across cable resistance: Vdrop = I × Rcable.
