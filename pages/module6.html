Module 6 · Long-term performance

6. Maintenance

40 min Requires Module 5 Active

Why maintenance matters

A solar system is a long-term investment designed to operate for 25–30 years. Without regular maintenance, energy yield degrades much faster than the manufacturer's warranty assumes. Dust, dirt, bird droppings and leaf deposits on the panel surface can reduce output by 10–30 % depending on climate and location.

Beyond soiling, the system's electrical connections, mounting hardware and inverter components are exposed to weather and thermal cycling year-round. Proactive inspection catches small problems — a loose connector, a cracked bypass diode, a corroding terminal — before they become costly failures or safety hazards.

📊 Industry data. A well-maintained system degrades at ~0.5 %/year; a neglected system at 1–2 %/year. Over 25 years, the cumulative difference in energy production can exceed 15 %.

Cleaning schedule

Panel cleaning frequency depends on local conditions. In dry, dusty climates monthly cleaning may be necessary. In wetter, windier climates with regular rainfall, quarterly cleaning is often sufficient — rain acts as a natural rinse.

Monthly Dusty / arid climates

Dry regions, near farmland or construction sites. Visible soiling accumulates quickly.

Quarterly Moderate climates

Regular rainfall helps. Clean before and after wet season for best results.

Annual Wet / coastal climates

High rainfall keeps panels relatively clean. Annual inspection still recommended.

Always clean panels in the early morning or late afternoon when they are cool. Use soft-bristle brushes or squeegees with de-ionised or low-mineral water to avoid leaving mineral deposits.

⚠️ Never use abrasive pads, high-pressure washers (above 35 bar) or harsh detergents. These can scratch the anti-reflective coating and void the panel warranty.

Electrical inspection checklist

Perform a thorough electrical inspection at least once a year. The checklist covers: panel connectors (MC4) — check for moisture ingress, corrosion and secure seating; string combiner and junction boxes — inspect for overheating marks and insulation damage; DC disconnect and AC breaker — verify operation and terminal torque; battery terminals — clean corrosion, check electrolyte level; inverter — review fault log, check cooling fan.

Use a clamp meter to verify that each string's short-circuit current matches the expected datasheet value. A string producing significantly less current than its neighbours may have a failed panel, shading issue or a degraded connector.

⚠️ Safety. DC circuits can carry lethal voltage even at night due to charged batteries. Always use appropriate PPE, insulated tools and a voltage tester before touching any DC conductor.

Shading analysis

Shading is one of the most insidious performance losses because it can be partial, seasonal and hard to detect without monitoring data. A shadow on a single cell reduces the output of the entire string it belongs to if no bypass diodes or power optimisers are present.

Common shading sources include trees that have grown since installation, new construction on neighbouring properties, satellite dishes, chimneys and seasonal changes in the sun's path. Use a solar pathfinder, a smartphone app, or monitoring software to identify shading periods.

🌡️ Thermal imaging is the gold standard for diagnosing shading and cell defects. A thermal camera reveals hot spots caused by mismatched or damaged cells, allowing targeted replacement before the defect causes fire or connector failure.

Long-term monitoring

Modern inverters include built-in data loggers accessible via Wi-Fi or a manufacturer's cloud portal. Monitor these key metrics daily:

kWh Daily energy yield
kWh/kWp Specific yield vs irradiance
>75 % Performance ratio (PR)
°C Inverter temperature
A / V String currents & voltages
SoC Battery state of charge

Set up automated alerts for: yield below 80 % of the rolling 30-day average; any inverter fault code; battery state-of-charge below minimum threshold. Early detection of a 5–8 % drop in specific yield can point to soiling, shading or a failed string.

📋 Keep a logbook — digital or paper — recording each inspection date, cleaning performed, faults observed and corrective actions. This record is invaluable for warranty claims and long-term health tracking.

Key terms

Soiling loss Energy loss due to dirt, dust or debris on the panel surface. Can reach 10–30 % without regular cleaning.
Degradation rate Annual percentage decline in panel power output. Well-maintained panels degrade at ~0.5 %/year.
Performance ratio (PR) Ratio of actual energy output to theoretical maximum given local irradiance. A healthy system targets PR > 75 %.
Hot spot Localised overheating in a cell caused by partial shading or damage. Detectable via thermal imaging.
Specific yield Annual energy produced per installed kilowatt-peak (kWh/kWp). The key metric for comparing system performance.
Bypass diode Diode integrated in the junction box that routes current around a shaded or faulty cell string within the panel.
